# Bacterial Leaf Blight in Rice (Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzae)

**Overview:**
Bacterial Leaf Blight (BLB) is one of the most destructive diseases affecting rice crops, particularly in warm, humid regions of Asia. It is caused by the bacterium *Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzae*.

**Symptoms:**
- Water-soaked to yellowish lesions appear on leaf margins starting near the tips.
- Lesions expand along the leaf margins with characteristic wavy edges, turning light yellow to grayish-white.
- Bacterial ooze droplets (milky dew drops) may be observed on young infected leaves during early morning.
- In severe infections ("kresek" stage), whole leaves dry up, turn straw-colored, and die.

**Favorable Conditions:**
- High temperature (25–34°C), high relative humidity (>70%), heavy rain, and windy weather.
- Excessive application of nitrogenous fertilizers.

**Management & Decision Support Steps:**
1. Remove and destroy severely infected leaves or plants to prevent field spread.
2. Avoid excessive nitrogen application; split N doses appropriately.
3. Apply copper oxychloride (3 g/L) mixed with Streptocycline or copper hydroxide spray at early onset.
4. Ensure proper field drainage to eliminate standing stagnant water.
